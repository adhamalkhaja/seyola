# LinkedIn Post Master Structure

A 5-step process for creating LinkedIn posts that shift perspectives and convert. Not random inspiration. A systematic workflow from raw idea to published post.

## The 5-Step Creation Process

### Step 1: Mind Dump To Articulate
Dump all ideas in raw form. Don't edit. Don't structure. Just get it out. What do you want to say? What triggered this thought? What frustrates you about this topic? What do you wish your audience understood?

Write messy. Write fast. Get 200-500 words of raw material. This is the clay.

### Step 2: Restructure To Shift Perspective
Now shape the clay. Apply the [[perspective-shifting-code]] framework:
- Identify the big idea (problem, outcome, or idea trigger)
- Build the Hell-Heaven-Bridge arc
- Find the perspective shift — what belief changes?
- Cut everything that doesn't serve the shift

Move from "here's what I know" to "here's what changes when you see it this way."

### Step 3: Deepen Hell, Heighten Heaven, Strengthen Bridge
Psychographic refinement. Go back into the hell section and make it more specific:
- Replace generic pain with specific day-to-day details
- Add exact emotions and internal dialogue
- Use phrases from actual client conversations
- Make them think "how does this person know my life?"

Then heighten heaven — make the future more vivid, specific, and tangible.

Then strengthen the bridge — make the insight crystal clear.

### Step 4: Choose Media
What visual or media accompaniment fits this post?
- **No media** — Pure text (works for short, punchy perspective shifts)
- **Image** — Selfie, workspace photo, screenshot (personal touch)
- **Carousel** — Multi-slide story or framework breakdown
- **Video** — Talking head, screen recording, day-in-the-life clip
- **Document** — PDF, infographic, chart

Rule: Media should enhance the post, not replace it. The text must stand alone. Media is a bonus.

### Step 5: Post & Analyze
Post and then track for 24 hours. Focus on money metrics only:

**24-Hour Post Analysis (what to track):**
- Who is commenting & what they say (ignore AI/generic comments)
- Who is liking (potential DM conversations)
- What emotion was triggered (did people share? save?)
- How many DMed you
- How many signed up to email list

**What to ignore:** Impressions (vanity), follower count (vanity), likes from random accounts (vanity). Focus on: saves, shares, DMs, and email signups.

## The 8 Writing Questions

Before writing any post, answer these:

1. **What is the big idea, outcome, or problem?** — One thing. Not three. One.
2. **Who exactly will I be writing this to?** — Not "entrepreneurs." A specific person at a specific stage.
3. **What does their current day-to-day look like?** — The psychographic reality. What's Monday morning like?
4. **What misconception do I want to shift?** — The belief that's keeping them stuck.
5. **What story or proof do I use to make them believe?** — From [[signature-stories]] or real experience.
6. **What first statement triggers them to "see more"?** — The hook. Test multiple options.
7. **How can I use the least amount of words to deliver the message?** — Every word earns its place. Cut ruthlessly.
8. **What phrases from clients or market can I use?** — Psychographic injection. Their words, not yours.

## First-Line Hook Strategies

The first line determines everything. If they don't click "see more," the rest doesn't exist.

### Problem Hook
Names a pain directly. "Most people treat LinkedIn like a lottery ticket." "Your content isn't failing because of the algorithm."

### Outcome Hook
Leads with a result. "I went from 200 to 5,000 followers in 90 days." "My client closed 3 deals from one post."

### Contrarian Hook
Challenges a common belief. "Posting every day is killing your brand." "Free value content is destroying your positioning."

### Validated Hook
Uses a proven hook from a high-performing post (80% hook, 20% your spin). Find posts that performed well for others in your niche. Take the structure, change the content. This is the [[growth-content]] "How I Steal To Grow" applied to writing.

**Hook rules:**
- First 5-7 words must create enough curiosity to keep reading
- Start with the payoff, not the setup
- Specific beats vague ("$22K in March" beats "more revenue")
- Questions work if they're questions the reader is already asking themselves

## Psychographic Injection Points

Where to inject audience-specific language:

- **In the hell section** — Their exact daily frustrations. "Staring at a blank post editor at 9 PM, knowing you need to be consistent but having nothing to say."
- **In the hook** — Their internal monologue. "You post. You wait. Crickets."
- **In the CTA** — Their desired outcome. "If you're ready to stop posting into the void..."

**Source material for psychographics:**
- DM conversations with leads
- Strategy call transcripts
- Client intake forms
- Reddit/forum posts from your niche
- Comments on competitor content

## Post Formatting Rules

LinkedIn is consumed on phones. Every post follows this visual structure:

### The hook (first 3 visible lines before "see more")

- **Line 1:** The hook — MAX 60 characters. This determines if they read or scroll.
- **Line 2:** Strategic whitespace (empty line)
- **Line 3:** Supporting punch — MAX 39 characters. Must NOT spill below the "see more" button.

### Body formatting

- **One sentence = one line** — This is the default rhythm
- **Strategic whitespace** (empty line) between every line
- **Occasionally 2 lines together** when the thought needs it — but this is the exception
- **Short sentences** — 5-12 words average
- **No walls of text** — Every line breathes
- **Specific numbers** — "539 coffees" not "many coffees"
- **Stories beat lectures** — Always
- **Emotional beats** — Pain → hope → direction (not logical progression)

### Style patterns

- Parenthetical asides for personality: "(information they don't need to know)"
- Bullet/dash lists only when listing specific items (not as default structure)
- Anaphora (repeated sentence starts) for emotional crescendo
- One-word or two-word sentences for punch: "Exactly.", "I'm done."
- Rhetorical questions that answer themselves
- CAPS for emphasis on key words (not whole sentences): "EINSTEIN-level"
- Quotes for reported speech
- No hashtags at the end

## The Content Mix (4-4-2 Ratio)

Out of every 10 LinkedIn posts:
- **4 Authority posts** — Conversion content: case studies, belief shifts, frameworks
- **4 Growth posts** — Steal/remix viral ideas, problem-naming, hot takes
- **2 Personality posts** — Personal stories, vices, behind-the-scenes, mundane moments

This ratio maps to the [[hummer-protocol]] 50/50 split (growth + conversion) with personal woven throughout.
