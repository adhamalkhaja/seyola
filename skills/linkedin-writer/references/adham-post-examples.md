# LinkedIn Post Examples

These are real, high-performing LinkedIn posts. Study the STRUCTURE, not just the content. Notice:
- Hook + whitespace + support line (3-line hook pattern)
- One sentence per line throughout
- Strategic whitespace (empty line) between every line
- Parenthetical asides for personality
- CAPS for emphasis on key words
- Bullet lists only when listing specific items
- No hashtags
- Closing lines that land with weight

---

## Example 1: The Coffee Meetings Post

I had 539 "coffees meetings" in 2025.

I'm done.

I don't know which guru told expats:

"have 3 coffees with them and then talk business"

Because I've realized I either end up getting pitched.

OR get that "sense" of a hidden agenda.

OR it becomes a clear "consult me for free as I distract you with a potential partnership".

Confession: I accepted some of those meetings only because I had a headache and needed coffee.

(information they don't need to know)

So the phrases I suggest you run away from:

- "Let's COLLABORATE" - collaborate on what?
- "Let's explore SYNERGIES" - synergy who?
- "Let's EXPLORE" - explore what?

These people want to "see what you do" but don't need it.

They want to "keep you in mind" but really don't care.

They want to "explore synergies" when there are no synergies.

It's just "look at me work" disguised as business development.

The coffee culture & tradition is beautiful.

The "coffee meeting" culture is a toxic version of it.

It's now being abused.

You don't need more coffee meetings.

You need fewer meetings with the right people.

Filter, then filter, then filter.

And protect your calendar.

The right "synergy" will come without the mandatory "coffee"

Your time is worth more than a Karak.

(this message is not for everyone of course)

### Structure notes
- **Hook:** "I had 539 'coffees meetings' in 2025." (42 chars)
- **Whitespace**
- **Support:** "I'm done." (9 chars — ultra short for punch)
- **Body:** One sentence per line, whitespace between each
- **List:** Dash bullets for specific phrases to avoid
- **Anaphora:** "They want to..." repeated 3x for rhythm
- **Parenthetical asides:** "(information they don't need to know)", "(this message is not for everyone of course)"
- **Close:** Specific cultural reference (Karak) for local resonance

## Example 2: The ChatGPT Post

Your audience prefers ChatGPT over paying you.

(your job is done - complete CINEMA)

The LinkedIn feed has become a ChatGPT chat.

- The posts are "correct" but they're empty
- The frameworks are clean but have no life
- The comments are long but say nothing

Then when engagement drops, they say "THE ALGO!".

People are not dumb, they "feel" when it's AI.

- "Not A. Not B. Not C."
- "It's not X, it's Y."
- "Here's the thing:"

And you know what else they feel?

That you don't care about their time.

You tell me, which one would you choose:

1. A book that's completely written with AI
2. A book that's handwritten

Exactly.

Another question:

How would you write an important email to a very important person?

You'd even handwrite if it wasn't awkward right?

Exactly "again".

That's why your content is not working.

AI is EINSTEIN-level genius.

But it'll never know the weight of opening LinkedIn with nothing to say but posting anyway because you can't afford to disappear.

It will never know what it's like to transfer your savings into a business while telling your family "it's going well".

It will never know what it feels like to smile on a discovery call while your bank account is screaming.

It will never know the loneliness of building something that nobody around you understands.

That's what gives "soul" to anything that comes from you.

(even "rewriting" with AI hurts you)

Be human, and stay human.

Otherwise, people would rather talk to GPT instead of you.

### Structure notes
- **Hook:** "Your audience prefers ChatGPT over paying you." (47 chars)
- **Whitespace**
- **Support:** "(your job is done - complete CINEMA)" (36 chars — parenthetical style)
- **Dash bullets:** Lists of specific AI patterns to call out
- **Numbered list:** For the "which would you choose" comparison
- **One-word sentences:** "Exactly." for punch
- **Anaphora:** "It will never know..." repeated 4x for emotional crescendo
- **CAPS emphasis:** "EINSTEIN-level", "THE ALGO!"
- **Multi-line exception:** The "It will never know" lines are longer — they're allowed to run because the anaphora rhythm carries them
- **Parenthetical closers:** "(even 'rewriting' with AI hurts you)"

## Example 3: The UAE Client Experience Post

If you want to learn client experience, study the UAE.

(BIG lesson from our government)

"Unreasonable Hospitality" is a popular book in the business world.

But there's a world-class playbook in front of you.

One that's been evolving for 15 years.

It's the UAE governmental services - most people don't know the effort that goes into this.

The Global Star Rating System launched in 2011.

(I recommend you read about it)

The aim was to rate service centers from 2 to 7 stars using independent audits & mystery shoppers.

While having the client (YOU) at the center of everything.

HH. Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid personally monitors this and calls them out publicly yearly.

He once saw a photo of long queues at Emirates Post.

His response:

"This is not our level. These are not our services. Whoever continues to provide such level will not be among my team"

He drove to Dubai Airport at night when baggage wait times hit 45 minutes.

And he spoke to passengers himself.

He then gave the airport 8 weeks to halve it.

All is to achieve the vision for 2071:

"Make the UAE the best country in the world"

And one HUGE pillar here is:

"The best government in the world"

And that'll only happen with ruthless action at all layers.

Now let's ask private businesses in the UAE:

Are you helping the 2071 vision with your business or hurting it?

I'll speak from what I see:

Most start by saying they care about the clients.

But in 6-12 months, it only becomes about money.

And they start complaining about everything.

Even when they read the hospitality books.

Even when they quote the frameworks.

When it comes to execution:

"Yalla, they already paid...get more clients"

They inherited old beliefs:

"They'll come back. Where else would they go?"

That's more مال اول energy.

This region has different standards now.

You honk your car and a Karak comes to your window.

Petrol stations have attendants waiting before you stop.

You walk into a mall and someone offers to carry your bags.

Government offices have better service than private clinics.

Don't let that all play with your head.

You have the responsibility to give back.

Here, we learn leadership from our leaders.

We learn hospitality from our government.

And we die contributing to our leaders' visions.

That's the Emirati code.

Learn from the country you're standing in.

So, I ask again:

"What's your contribution to the 2071 vision of this country?"

### Structure notes
- **Hook:** "If you want to learn client experience, study the UAE." (54 chars)
- **Whitespace**
- **Support:** "(BIG lesson from our government)" (31 chars — parenthetical)
- **Long-form storytelling:** Same one-line-per-sentence format, just more lines
- **Quotes:** Direct speech in quotation marks for leader quotes
- **Anaphora:** "Even when they..." repeated, "We learn..." repeated
- **Cultural references:** Arabic phrase "مال اول", "Karak", specific UAE references
- **Rhetorical questions:** "Are you helping the 2071 vision with your business or hurting it?"
- **Callback close:** Returns to the opening theme with "So, I ask again:"
- **Specific details:** "2011", "45 minutes", "8 weeks", "2071" — numbers ground the story
