# Automation & Segmentation

## The Pattern: Trigger → Tag → Sequence

Every automation follows this pattern. Dead-simple. Don't overcomplicate it.

- **Trigger** — Something happens (form submission, purchase, click)
- **Tag** — A label gets applied to the subscriber
- **Sequence** — An email sequence fires automatically

Kit's visual automation builder makes this drag-and-drop. No code.

## Lead Magnet Automation

**Trigger:** Someone joins your landing page form
**Tag:** "mini-course-subscriber"
**Action:** Start the mini-course [[welcome-sequence-framework|welcome sequence]]

This is your first automation. Set it up, test it with your own email, verify it works end-to-end. Then leave it running forever.

## High-Intent Automation

**Trigger:** LinkedIn comment or waitlist form
**Tag:** "77" (high-intent signal)
**Action:** Trigger offer doc sequence (usually 1 email with your offer document PDF link)

Why "77"? It's short, memorable, and signals "this person raised their hand." You'll use this tag to identify your warmest leads.

## The LinkedIn-to-Kit Pipeline

Full flow — runs automatically once set up:

1. You publish a post with a comment trigger: "Comment GUIDE and I'll DM you"
2. Someone comments "GUIDE"
3. You DM them (manually or via Taplio — never auto-DM, violates ToS): "Here's the link: [landing page]"
4. They click, opt in on landing page
5. Kit applies the "77" tag automatically
6. Offer doc sequence fires — they get your [[magnet-stack|best offer]] in their inbox

The DM is personal. The rest is automated. This is your highest-converting pipeline because every person in it took a public action.

## The 2 Essential Tags

Keep it stupid simple. Only 2 tags.

### 1. Client Tag
Applied when someone purchases + submits onboarding form. Zapier connects your payment processor to Kit.

**Purpose:** Exclude from sales sequences. Once they're a client, stop pitching them and start serving them.

### 2. High-Intent Tag "77"
Applied from LinkedIn comments, waitlist forms, direct engagement signals.

**Purpose:** Identify your warmest leads. These people took action — they commented publicly, joined a waitlist, or engaged directly. They're not just subscribers, they're prospects.

### The Broadcast Philosophy

**Broadcast to your ENTIRE list.** Let content relevance do the segmentation. People who aren't interested just won't open. That's fine.

Why this works: Simple = more emails sent. More consistency = higher revenue. Complex segmentation = fewer emails = lower revenue. Most people who build complex segment trees end up sending LESS email, not more.

Your [[pfc-avatar-layers|PFC]] is already specific enough. If your list is built around one niche, everyone on it is roughly the right audience.

## Super Signature

Strategic CTAs at key moments — not in every email.

**2-3 options:**
1. Subscribe to YouTube (deep link via LinkTwin opens YouTube app)
2. Listen to Podcast/Spotify (deep link opens app directly)
3. See how we can work together (branded landing page link)

**Placement:** Intro email + final summary email of your mini-course ONLY. Not every daily lesson. Subtle feels like options, not a sales pitch.

**Deep links via LinkTwin:** Opens apps directly instead of browser. Better for algorithm signals on YouTube and Spotify.

Update quarterly as your CTAs evolve — new podcast? New offer? Swap it in.

## Start Simple

One automation. Get it working. Test with your own email. Verify every step fires correctly. Then add the second one.

Don't build 5 automations on Day 1. You'll spend weeks debugging instead of sending emails.
