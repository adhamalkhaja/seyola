# Pricing & Payment Strategy

## The Pricing Seesaw

Pricing is a seesaw based on two things:
- What the market is willing to spend
- Your confidence level

In the beginning, [[freedom-number-methodology|confidence]] is the bottleneck, not the market.

"There is no strategic advantage to being the second most expensive in the market. There IS to being the most expensive." — [[hormozi-offer|Alex Hormozi]]

## Three Pricing Models

### 1. PAY IN ADVANCE (PIF)
One payment. Maximum cash upfront. Offer 20-30% savings vs split pay. Dream model but requires proof and confidence to sell.

### 2. SPLIT PAY (recommended up to $50K/month)
3-month or 5-month split. Highest cashflow-producing model. Offer a PIF upgrade within 30-60 days.

Math example:
- 5-month split: $1,500 × 5 = $7,500
- 3-month split: $2,500 × 3 = $7,500
- PIF (save 20%): $6,000

### 3. MONTHLY RECURRING (MRR)
Only consider after $50K/month cash collected. More predictable but 6-9 month cashflow catch-up period. Higher churn risk. You are the bank.

## Payment Processor Rotation

Stripe and PayPal are HIGH-RISK for coaching. They can freeze funds with zero warning.

**Set up 3+ processors:** Stripe, EasyPay Direct, Skool, WAP, Klarna. Rotate payments evenly across them. Never rely on just one.

## Key Rules

- Avoid weekly payments and cancel-whenever models (attract toe-dippers)
- Always optimize for cash upfront
- Rise price quarterly (2-5%, no more than 4× per year)
- Price based on the transformation, not the time — see [[offer-blueprint-components|offer blueprint]] for full structure
- Set up missed payment notifications + dunning process
