# Conversion & Launch Sequences

## Conversion Sequence (4-7 Emails)

The conversion sequence is for evergreen selling — no cart deadlines, no launch dates. It runs when someone is ready (typically after completing your [[welcome-sequence-framework|welcome sequence]]) and walks them from awareness to action.

### The Arc: OPEN → DESIRE → PROOF → OBJECTION → URGENCY → CLOSE

**Email 1: Open**
Introduce the offer. State the core promise. Set the frame for what's possible. This is NOT a hard sell — it's a "here's what exists and why it matters."

Use the [[one-one-one-framework|111 Framework]]: one big idea (the core promise), one story (a client who achieved it), one action (learn more).

**Email 2: Desire**
Paint the transformation. Show the gap between where they are and where they could be. Make it visceral — not abstract. Specific numbers, specific timelines, specific outcomes.

"Sarah went from 3K/month and working 60-hour weeks to 28K/month and taking Fridays off. In 4 months."

**Email 3: Proof**
Stack the evidence. This is your testimonial email. Real names. Real results. Real screenshots from the Be Proud folder. Multiple examples — different starting points, different niches, same system.

The more specific the proof, the more believable: "$47,329 in Month 3" beats "made great money."

**Email 4: Objection**
Handle the biggest "but..." proactively. What's the #1 reason someone would NOT buy? Address it head-on. Don't dodge it. Name it, empathize with it, and then reframe it.

Common objections: "I don't have time," "I've tried things before," "Is this right for my niche?" Pick the most common one and dedicate the entire email to it.

**Email 5: Urgency (if authentic)**
Only include this if you have a real reason for urgency — price increase, limited spots, cohort closing. If there's no authentic urgency, skip this email entirely. Fake urgency destroys trust.

**Email 6-7: Close + Last Call**
Make the final ask. Summarize everything: the transformation, the proof, the objection handling. One clear CTA. If there's a deadline, the last email is a "doors close tonight" reminder.

### Timing

Standard: every 2 days between emails. Daily during active promos. Don't send conversion emails back-to-back same day — give each one breathing room.

## Launch Sequence (6-10 Emails)

The launch sequence is for time-bound campaigns — product launches, cohort opens, seasonal promos. It's more intense than conversion because there's a real deadline.

### The Timeline

**Pre-Launch (Day -3, Day -1)**

Two emails before cart opens:

- **Day -3: Seed.** Plant the idea. "Something's coming." Don't reveal everything. Open a curiosity loop. Tease the transformation without naming the product.
- **Day -1: Anticipation.** Share the backstory. Why you built this. Who it's for (and who it's not for). Give them a reason to watch for tomorrow's email.

**Cart Open (Day 0)**

Two emails on open day:

- **Morning: Announcement.** Here it is. What's included, who it's for, the price, the link. Clear and complete.
- **Evening: Different Angle.** Same offer, different story. Maybe a client result. Maybe a personal story about why this matters. NOT a repeat of the morning email.

**Mid-Launch (Day 2-5)**

The [[email-cadence|promo week]] daily themes apply here:

- **Day 2: Problem + Belief Shift.** Agitate the pain. Challenge an assumption they hold.
- **Day 3: Proof.** Case study. Real results. Specific numbers.
- **Day 4: Plan.** Walk them through what happens when they join. The roadmap. Remove uncertainty.
- **Day 5: Principle.** The philosophy behind the offer. WHY it works. The methodology.

Each mid-launch email uses the [[one-one-one-framework|111 Framework]] — one idea per email, one story, one action. Don't try to cram multiple angles into one email.

**Cart Close (Day 6-7)**

The urgency is real and earned:

- **48 hours:** "Doors close in 2 days. Here's what you'll miss."
- **24 hours:** "Last day. Here's the question to ask yourself."
- **Final hours:** "Closing tonight at midnight. Last chance."
- **Last call:** "Doors are closed. [If applicable: waitlist link for next time.]"

## Key Principles

**Value-to-pitch ratio matters.** For low-ticket offers ($50-200), a shorter conversion sequence works (4 emails). For high-ticket ($2K+), you need more trust-building (6-7 emails). The higher the price, the more proof and objection-handling required.

**Don't skip the proof email.** It's the most important email in both sequences. People buy from people who have helped people like them.

**One CTA per email.** Even in a launch. Especially in a launch. Multiple links split attention and reduce action.
