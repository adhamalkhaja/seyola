# Nurture, Re-engagement & Post-Purchase Sequences

## Nurture Sequence (Ongoing)

Not everyone buys on the first pass. Most people need 90 days to 2 years before they're ready. The nurture sequence keeps you valuable and visible during that window.

### Purpose

Stay in the inbox without being annoying. Provide enough value that when they ARE ready to buy, you're the obvious choice. No hard selling — just consistent presence.

### Structure

The nurture sequence is your regular [[email-cadence|broadcast cadence]] in automated form. Use the [[one-one-one-framework|111 Framework]] for every email: one big idea, one story, one action.

**Content sources:**
- Previously-written broadcast emails (repurposed into the automated sequence)
- YouTube video transcripts turned into emails (the Inception Flywheel from [[magnet-stack|magnet stack]])
- Client wins from the Be Proud folder
- Personal stories, insights, hot takes

**Cadence:** Every 2-3 days. This extends automated contact to 40+ days beyond the [[welcome-sequence-framework|welcome sequence]] without writing anything new.

### Themes (following the non-promo week pattern)

Rotate through three themes:

1. **Problem-focused** — Paint a pain point, link to a YouTube video that addresses it
2. **Principle/insight** — Share a perspective shift, a contrarian take, a belief worth challenging
3. **Client win story** — Screenshot from the Be Proud folder, tell the story, celebrate the result

### Transition to Broadcasts

Once the automated nurture sequence ends (Day 40-60+), subscribers transition to your live broadcast list. They now receive whatever you're sending to everyone else — same cadence, same content, same rhythm.

## Re-engagement Sequence (3-4 Emails)

People go cold. It happens. They get busy, change priorities, lose interest. The re-engagement sequence is a last-chance effort before you remove them from your list.

### Trigger

Run this when someone hasn't opened a single email in 90 days. This is a behavioral trigger in your [[sequence-architecture|automation]], not a manual process.

### The 3-4 Email Pattern

**Email 1: Pattern Interrupt**

Subject: Something completely different from your normal style. "We need to talk." or "Should I stop emailing you?"

Body: Short. Personal. "I noticed you haven't opened my emails in a while. I get it — inboxes are noisy. But I want to make sure I'm only showing up for people who want me here."

CTA: "Click here if you still want to hear from me."

The click re-tags them as engaged and removes them from the re-engagement sequence.

**Email 2: Pure Value (Day 3)**

Subject: Your absolute best content. The email you're most proud of.

Body: Deliver massive value with zero pitch. This is your best shot at proving you're worth keeping. Use your strongest [[one-one-one-framework|story]], your most impactful insight.

CTA: Soft — link to a resource, video, or tool. No selling.

**Email 3: Direct Question (Day 6)**

Subject: "Are you still interested in [specific result]?"

Body: The 9-word email format. Ultra-short. "Hey — are you still looking to [achieve specific result]? If so, reply YES and I'll make sure you keep getting my best stuff."

CTA: Reply YES.

**Email 4: Final / Goodbye (Day 9)**

Subject: "Removing you from the list"

Body: "I'm cleaning up my list and I'm going to remove people who haven't engaged in a while. If you want to stay, click the link below. If not, no hard feelings — I wish you all the best."

CTA: "Keep me on the list" link.

### The Psychology

The "removing you" email is the highest-converting email in the re-engagement sequence. People don't want to lose access to something, even if they haven't used it. Loss aversion is real.

If they don't click, **remove them**. A clean list with 5,000 engaged subscribers beats a dirty list with 15,000. Dead subscribers hurt your [[esp-deliverability|sender reputation]] and tank your metrics for the people who DO want your emails.

## Post-Purchase Sequence (4-6 Emails)

The relationship doesn't end at purchase. It deepens. The post-purchase sequence turns a buyer into a success story, and success stories become your best marketing.

### The 6-Email Arc

**Email 1: Welcome + Onboard (Day 0)**

Immediate. Celebrate their decision. Give them everything they need to get started — login links, community access, first steps. Remove all friction.

CTA: Complete the first quick win within 24 hours.

**Email 2: Quick Win (Day 2)**

Guide them to their first result. The faster someone sees a result, the more committed they become. Pick the simplest, fastest win in your program.

**Email 3: Community Invite (Day 5)**

Introduce them to the community or support system. Peer connections increase retention and results. "You're not doing this alone."

**Email 4: Check-In (Day 10)**

Personal. "How's it going? Hit reply if you're stuck on anything." This email catches people who are struggling silently and prevents drop-off.

**Email 5: Upsell Bridge (Day 20)**

Once they've had results, introduce the next level. NOT a hard sell — a natural "here's what's next when you're ready." This only works if they've actually gotten value from the first purchase.

**Email 6: Referral Ask (Day 30)**

"Know someone who'd benefit from this? Forward this email or share the link." Happy clients are your best growth channel. Ask explicitly — most people won't think to refer unless prompted.

### Tag Management

When someone purchases, the [[automation-segmentation|Client tag]] is applied via Zapier. This automatically:
- Removes them from any active sales/conversion sequences
- Starts the post-purchase sequence
- Excludes them from future sales broadcasts (they already bought)
