# Welcome Sequence Framework

## The Arc: DELIVER → CONNECT → VALUE → BRIDGE → PITCH

The welcome sequence is the most important sequence you'll ever build. First impressions compound. Someone who has a great first week with your emails becomes a reader for months. Someone who gets a generic "thanks for subscribing" email and then silence? Gone.

This framework gives each email a specific job in a 7-email progression over 12 days. Every email earns the right to send the next one.

## The 7-Email Breakdown

### Email 1: Deliver (Day 0)

**Job:** Give them what they came for. Immediately.

They opted in for a reason — your [[magnet-stack|lead magnet]] promised something. Deliver it in the first email. No teasing. No "it's on its way." Give them the thing.

**Structure:**
- Subject: Direct reference to what they signed up for
- Body: Here's what you asked for + what to expect next
- CTA: Reply with a password word (the [[esp-deliverability|password reply hack]] — "Reply MANGO to unlock full access")

The reply CTA is critical. One reply marks you as a trusted sender forever. This is your [[esp-deliverability|deliverability]] foundation.

### Email 2: Connect (Day 2)

**Job:** Build rapport. Share your story. Make them feel like they know you.

This is not a teaching email. It's a "here's who I am and why I care about this" email. Share the short version of your journey — where you started, what you struggled with, why you do this work.

**Structure:**
- Subject: Personal, conversational (not educational)
- Body: Your story in 3-5 paragraphs. Raw, real, imperfect.
- CTA: Soft — "Reply and tell me where you're at right now"

### Email 3: Value (Day 4)

**Job:** Teach something useful. Create a quick win.

Pick ONE concept from your methodology and explain it clearly. Something they can implement today and see a result. Quick wins build trust faster than any amount of theory.

**Structure:**
- Subject: Promise a specific result
- Body: The concept + how to apply it + what to expect
- CTA: "Try this today and reply with what happened"

### Email 4: More Value (Day 6)

**Job:** Different angle, more depth, builds authority.

Take a different angle on your expertise. If Email 3 was tactical, make this one strategic. If Email 3 was about doing, make this one about thinking differently. The [[one-one-one-framework|111 Framework]] works perfectly here — one idea, one story, one action.

**Structure:**
- Subject: Contrarian take or unexpected insight
- Body: Belief shift + supporting story + practical takeaway
- CTA: Link to a relevant YouTube video or resource

### Email 5: Bridge (Day 8)

**Job:** Show the gap between where they are and where they could be.

This is the transition from "free value" to "here's how to go further." Not a pitch yet. An honest acknowledgment that there's more to the story than what a free sequence can cover.

**Structure:**
- Subject: Question that highlights the gap
- Body: "Here's what you've learned so far... and here's what most people get stuck on next"
- CTA: Tease what's coming — "Tomorrow I'm going to share something I usually only share with clients"

### Email 6: Soft Pitch (Day 10)

**Job:** Introduce the offer naturally. Handle the biggest objection.

Now they've gotten 5 emails of value. They know you, trust you, and have seen results. Introduce your offer as the logical next step — not a sales pitch, but a "here's how to go deeper."

**Structure:**
- Subject: Curiosity-based (not salesy)
- Body: The transformation your offer delivers + one specific client result + the biggest objection answered proactively
- CTA: "Here's everything you need to know" → link to offer doc or sales page

### Email 7: Direct Pitch (Day 12)

**Job:** Make the clear ask. No ambiguity.

If they've opened 6 emails from you over 12 days, they're interested. Respect that interest with a clear, direct invitation.

**Structure:**
- Subject: Direct and honest ("Ready to [specific result]?")
- Body: Recap the transformation + social proof + clear CTA
- CTA: ONE link. Application page, booking link, or offer doc. Not three options.

## Timing Principles

| Email | Day | Gap | Why |
|-------|-----|-----|-----|
| 1. Deliver | 0 | — | Immediate. Don't make them wait. |
| 2. Connect | 2 | 2 days | Let them consume the lead magnet first |
| 3. Value | 4 | 2 days | Consistent rhythm |
| 4. More Value | 6 | 2 days | Building momentum |
| 5. Bridge | 8 | 2 days | Transition point |
| 6. Soft Pitch | 10 | 2 days | They're warm now |
| 7. Direct Pitch | 12 | 2 days | Clear ask |

After Day 12, subscribers flow into your [[nurture-reengagement|nurture sequence]] or regular broadcast cadence.

## Subject Line Direction

Each email in the welcome sequence should use a different [[subject-line-formulas|subject line formula]]. Don't repeat the same pattern twice in a row. The welcome sequence is where people form their impression of your emails — variety keeps them opening.
